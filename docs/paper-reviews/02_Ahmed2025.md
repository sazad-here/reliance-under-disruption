## 1 SNAPSHOT

- **Paper:** Ahmed, K.R., Ansari, M.E., Ahsan, M.N., Rohan, A., Uddin, M.B., Rivin, M.A.H. "Deep learning framework for interpretable supply chain forecasting using SOM ANN and SHAP." *Scientific Reports* 15:26355 (2025).
- **Problem:** predict (a) shipping duration (regression) and (b) late-delivery risk (binary classification) on the DataCo Smart Supply Chain dataset.
- **Method:** PCA (and Truncated SVD) → SOM clustering → ANN/BPNN whose architecture and weights are tuned by a genetic algorithm; SHAP for post-hoc explanation.
- **Data:** DataCo Global (Kaggle), 180,519 rows × 53 columns, 2015–2019 (Dataset 1); an unnamed Kaggle "Supply Chain DataSet", 15,549 rows × 46 columns (Dataset 2).
- **Headline numbers:** regression R² 0.92, RMSE 0.936, MAE 0.8459; classification accuracy 96 %, F1 96.22 % (Dataset 1); accuracy 89.65 % (Dataset 2).
- **Code/data:** data links given; **no code, no seeds, no environment**.
- [Assessment] The engineering claim is ordinary; the reporting is not internally consistent enough for the numbers to be taken at face value.

## 2 ABSTRACT

- **Claims:** SOM+ANN beats RF/XGBoost/DT on both tasks; R² 0.92 / RMSE 0.936 / MAE 0.8459; accuracy 96 %, F1 96.22 %; a second dataset gives 89.65 %; SHAP "enabled end users to comprehend how the model makes classification decisions."
- **Evidence in the abstract:** point estimates only. No baseline for the second dataset, no uncertainty, no dataset split protocol, no majority-class floor.
- [Assessment] Every abstract number is traceable to a table, which is good. But "much more accurate than conventional ML" is stated without any test of difference, and the abstract does not say the DataCo late-delivery label is majority class ≈ 57 %, so 96 % is not placed against a floor. The phrase "the research also employed another dataset, making it more accurate, better generalized, and robust" is a non-sequitur: adding an evaluation set does not make a model more accurate.

## 3 INTRODUCTION AND MOTIVATION

- **Claim:** traditional ML "require[s] a lot of feature engineering and face[s] high dimensions, unstructured, and even time series characterization" (p. 2), which DL overcomes.
- **Evidence:** citation-only; no measurement in this paper supports it. Notably, the paper's own pipeline is heavily feature-engineered (label encoding, standardisation, SVD, PCA, SOM cluster features) before the ANN sees anything, which contradicts the stated motivation.
- **Objectives:** four, quoted in the scratchpad. Objective 2 ("improve demand forecasting accuracy") is never addressed — no demand forecasting is performed anywhere in the paper.
- [Assessment] The gap statement is generic ("data fragmentation, computational complexity, interpretability … prevent the large-scale adoption of DL in SCM", p. 2) and is not connected to the two tasks actually solved. Objective 2 is an unmet promise, and the "Critical interpretation" section (p. 24) nonetheless asserts Objective 2 is achieved "By collecting intricate temporal-spatial patterns that are essential to precise demand forecasting" — which no result supports.

## 4 LITERATURE REVIEW / RELATED WORK

- **Structure:** "Predictive analytics in SCM" (Bag et al.²¹, Dubey et al.²², Wang et al.²³, Benzidia et al.²⁴) and "DL applications in supply chain forecasting" (MK et al.²⁵ CNN-LSTM, Jahin et al.²⁶ MCDFN, Ahn et al.²⁷ GNN), then six numbered gaps.
- **Evidence:** the first sub-section reviews *survey/SEM* studies (self-reported questionnaire data) which are not comparable to the paper's own predictive task; the review notes this itself ("self-reported data from questionnaires is unreliable", p. 3).
- **Miscitation:** the text at p. 3 attributes the study of 168 French hospitals to "Bonnes et al.²⁴"; reference 24 is **Benzidia, Makaoui & Bentahar** (2021). The name "Bonnes" appears nowhere in the reference list.
- **Gaps claimed:** six, including "Lack of Explainability and Transparency in AI Models" and "Computational Complexity of DL Models".
- [Assessment] The review does not cover the one literature that matters for the claim: prior DataCo late-delivery studies and what features they used. Those appear only later, in Table 6, without methodological description. Gap 4 (computational cost blocks SME adoption) is raised and then never addressed — no training time, no inference latency, no model size is reported anywhere.

## 5 METHODOLOGY

- **Pipeline (p. 3–12):** missing values "removing records with incomplete values or imputing missing values with zeros … The choice depended on the perceived significance and pattern of the missing data" (p. 5) → Label Encoding → StandardScaler → Truncated SVD → 80/20 `train_test_split()` → PCA → SOM → ANN (GA-tuned) → SHAP.
- **PCA:** reduced to 10 components retaining 78.4 % variance (p. 8, eq. 16).
- **SOM:** 10×10 rectangular grid, Gaussian neighbourhood, initial radius 5, learning rate 0.5→0.01, 100 epochs, batch training (p. 10, Table 1).
- **ANN:** 4 input neurons, 1 hidden layer 10–50 neurons (GA-selected), 2 softmax outputs, ReLU, Adam lr 0.001, batch 32, 100 epochs, dropout + early stopping (p. 12, Table 1).
- **GA:** population 20, crossover 0.8, mutation 0.01; fitness = `max{x_f/x_a, x̃_f/x̃_a}` (eq. 19).
- **Split integrity claim (p. 20):** "20% of the dataset was set aside for testing and 80% for training. These subsets were carefully examined to ensure no overlap or data leaked."

**Problems, each with a location:**

1. **The feature set is never stated.** Nowhere does the paper list which of the 53 DataCo columns are inputs. Given that the *entire* leakage question for this dataset turns on `Days for shipping (real)` and `Delivery Status`, this is the single most important omission. Not stated in paper.
2. **The pipeline order does not match the ordering claims.** Preprocessing applies Truncated SVD (p. 5) *and* PCA (p. 5, 8) *and* SOM. Whether SOM runs on PCA output or on SVD output is never resolved.
3. **Preprocessing is fit before the split.** The split is described at p. 5 as the *last* preprocessing step ("Finally, the dataset was divided into training (80%) and testing (20%)"), after StandardScaler, SVD and PCA. Fitting the scaler and the decompositions on all 180,519 rows leaks test-set distribution into training. The p. 20 claim of "no overlap or data leaked" addresses row overlap only, not fitted-transform leakage.
4. **Eq. (19) selects on the test set.** The GA fitness is the **maximum** of training and *testing* accuracy. The test set therefore drives model selection, so the reported test accuracy is an optimistically biased selection statistic, not a held-out estimate. This directly contradicts p. 20's "objectively assessing its effectiveness."
5. **4 input neurons vs 10 principal components** (Fig. 6, Table 1, p. 11–12 vs p. 8). The two cannot both be true.
6. **Loss function stated twice, differently** (Table 1 categorical CE vs p. 12 binary CE).
7. **SOM grid stated as 10×10** but Fig. 3 renders roughly 15×10.
8. **Boilerplate from another domain:** "the business's default risk and credit rating are used as the output" (p. 11, twice).
9. **No class-imbalance handling and no majority floor.** Acknowledged only for Dataset 2 (Limitations, p. 24).
10. **No repeated runs, no seed, no confidence intervals** for Table 2–5. The 95 % CIs in Fig. 18 appear without a stated method.

- [Assessment] Points 3 and 4 are, on their own, enough to make the headline accuracy uninterpretable as a generalisation estimate — independently of the feature-leakage question our own project raised.

## 6 RESULTS

**Regression (shipping duration), Table 3, p. 17:**

| Model | MAE | MSE | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RF | 1.0463 | 1.7000 | 1.304 | 0.85 |
| XGBoost | 1.0487 | 1.7011 | 1.304 | 0.84 |
| DT | 1.0462 | 1.6999 | 1.304 | 0.84 |
| SOM+ANN | 0.8459 | 0.8767 | 0.936 | 0.92 |

- **Evidence check:** RMSE = √MSE holds throughout (√1.70 = 1.304; √0.8767 = 0.936) ✅. Back-solving the target variance from R² = 1 − MSE/Var gives ≈ 11.3 (RF), ≈ 10.6 (XGBoost, DT) and ≈ 11.0 (proposed) — a spread that two-decimal rounding of R² can account for, so this is not by itself an error, only a reminder that R² is reported too coarsely to check.
- **Not stated:** the regression feature set; whether `Days for shipment (scheduled)` was an input (Fig. 13's legend suggests both real and scheduled days are plotted as *actuals*, which is incoherent for a single-target regression).

**Classification (late-delivery risk):**
- Table 2 (ablation): Baseline ANN 92.81 acc; PCA+ANN 94.32; SOM+ANN 96 / 96.23 / 96.20 / 96.22.
- Table 4 (generalisation): RF 94.62→82.11; XGBoost 96.75→86.42; DT 96.77→83.11; SOM+ANN 98.65→96.
- Table 5: RF 82.54/81.23/81.88; DT 83.65/84.34/84.00; XGBoost 86.54/85.44/85.99; SOM+ANN 96.23/96.20/96.22.
- Table 6 (vs prior work): CNN-LSTM 92.60 (Douaioui et al.⁵⁵); KNN 85.42 (Bassiouni et al.⁵⁶); MLP 80.40 (Zheng et al.⁵⁷); ANN 72.23 (Söderholm⁵⁸); RF AUC 0.98 and 0.80 (Thomas & Panicker⁵⁹,⁶⁰); Proposed 96 / AUC 0.98.
- Table 7 (Dataset 2): precision 88.76, recall 88.42, F1 88.60, accuracy 89.65.
- Table 8: Kappa/MCC 0.97/0.96 (D1), 0.88/0.88 (D2).

**Evidence problems:**
- **AUC 0.98 is reported in Table 6 for the proposed model but computed and shown nowhere else.** No ROC curve, no AUC in Tables 2–5 or 7. Unverifiable from the paper.
- **97.56 % (p. 22)** contradicts Table 6's 96 and drives the "4.96% lesser" comparison against CNN-LSTM. Using Table 6's own 96, the gap would be 3.4 points.
- **94 % (p. 20)** contradicts Fig. 14, whose cells give 96.05 %.
- **Baselines get no tuning description.** RF/XGBoost/DT hyperparameters are not given, so "much more accurate than conventional ML" is a comparison against unspecified defaults.
- **No statistical test between models.** The only inferential content is the Fig. 18 CIs and Table 8's Kappa/MCC — neither compares models.
- [Assessment] The classification story is coherent in shape (proposed > XGBoost > DT > RF) but the numbers are transcribed inconsistently across Tables 2/5/6 and the body, and the single strongest comparison in the paper (Table 6, against published work) rests on a number (97.56 %) that appears in no table.

The two off-diagonal cells being *exactly equal* (742 = 742) is an unusual coincidence that the paper does not comment on. The proposed model takes 4 PCA components. Either the interpretability section explains a different model from the one benchmarked, or the "4 input neurons" specification is wrong. Both readings undermine the paper's central interpretability claim ("The agreement between this feature importance and SHAP further validates the interpretability of the model", p. 21).

The CI method is not stated; for n = 36,104 a normal-approximation CI around 0.96 would be roughly ±0.002, not ±0.011 — so the interval width is also unexplained.
- **Table 1 (p. 12).** Conflicts with Fig. 6 (hidden size) and with p. 12 (loss function), as tabulated in the scratchpad.
- **Table 2 (p. 17) Ablation.** Three rows for a four-variant ablation described in the text. No PCA+SOM+ANN row exists.
- **Table 3 (p. 17) Regression comparison.** RF, XGBoost and DT are within 0.003 MAE of each other — three very different model families landing on essentially identical errors is itself a warning sign of a trivial or degenerate target. Not commented on.
- **Table 4 (p. 19).** Caption duplicated from Table 3 and wrong for its content. - **Table 5 (p. 19).** Consistent with Table 2's "Proposed" row.
- **Table 6 (p. 22) Comparative analysis.** Two of six prior entries use different datasets (Söderholm⁵⁸ postal services; Zheng et al.⁵⁷ three engineering buyers); the paper says so but still tabulates them side-by-side. Accuracy and AUC columns are half-empty. No prior study's feature set or protocol is described, so "outperformed every study published" (p. 22) is a comparison of numbers, not of methods.
- **Table 7 (p. 22).** Dataset 2 metrics only; no baselines on Dataset 2, so the generalisation claim has no comparator.
- **Table 8 (p. 24).** Header unit "(%)" with values 0.88–0.97.

## 8 DISCUSSION

- **Claim (p. 24–25):** the four objectives are met; PCA+SOM "decreased noise and maintained the data's inherent topological correlations"; GA "mitigates … becoming stuck in local minima"; SHAP + U-Matrix "adds explainability".
- **Evidence:** the ablation (Table 2) supports "PCA helps over baseline ANN" and "SOM helps over PCA" for a single run each. Nothing in the paper measures noise, topology preservation, or GA escape from local optima (no fitness curves, no seeds, no comparison against BPNN without GA).
- **Discussion of Objective 2 (demand forecasting)** asserts the model "collect[s] intricate temporal-spatial patterns that are essential to precise demand forecasting" — no demand forecast is produced anywhere in the paper.
- [Assessment] The discussion restates results and attributes them to mechanisms that were not tested. The one genuinely interesting result — the small train/test gap in Table 4 — is credited to SOM feature extraction when the GA fitness function offers a simpler explanation.

## 9 LIMITATIONS

The paper's own list (p. 24), condensed: only two datasets; Dataset 2 imbalanced and no balancing applied; SOM scalability; "the ANN architecture was fixed and pre-defined for all tests, missing dynamic optimization or hyperparameter tuning particular to the datasets"; SHAP alone may not capture nonlinear interactions; no external temporal/contextual variables; no operational deployment test.

- **Internal contradiction:** "the ANN architecture was fixed and pre-defined for all tests, missing dynamic optimization or hyperparameter tuning" directly contradicts the headline contribution "we use GA to simultaneously optimize the ANN's architecture and weights" (p. 4).
- **Limitations the paper does not acknowledge:** the undefined feature set; preprocessing fitted before the split; GA fitness reading the test set; the absence of a majority-class floor (DataCo's late rate is ≈ 57 %, so 96 % must be read against ~57 %, not 50 %); no repeated runs; no significance testing; the Fig. 2 / eq. (16) contradiction; the 37,588-row test set.
- [Assessment] The limitations section is boilerplate-shaped and omits every issue that would change how the results are read.

## 10 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

- **Claim (p. 25):** the framework "showed some encouraging performance in shipping duration and slow shipping risk prediction"; SHAP addressed the black-box issue; future work = real-time data streams, more industries, operational trials.
- **Consistency:** the conclusion is markedly weaker than the abstract and the comparative-analysis section (97.56 %, "outperformed every study published"). It quietly drops the numbers.
- [Assessment] The conclusion is the most defensible part of the paper. Future work is generic and does not name the obvious next step for this dataset — an order-time-only feature set.

## 12 CRITICAL EVALUATION

**Strengths (max 3)**
1. **A real ablation is attempted** (Table 2) and a **second, independent dataset is evaluated** (Table 7, Fig. 17), with Dataset 2's confusion matrix internally consistent. Most papers in this niche do neither.
2. **Interpretability is reported, not just promised** — SHAP (Fig. 15) and global importance (Fig. 16) agree with each other, and both point at shipping mode and day-of-week rather than at financial features.
3. **The train/test gap is reported for every model** (Table 4), which is more transparency than the competing entries in Table 6 offer.

**Weaknesses (max 3)**
1. **The reported accuracy is not a held-out estimate.** The GA fitness is `max{train accuracy, test accuracy}` (eq. 19, p. 11), and scaling/SVD/PCA are described as fitted before the 80/20 split (p. 5). Both route test information into model selection, and the paper's assurance that "no overlap or data leaked" (p. 20) addresses neither.
2. **The feature set is never disclosed**, so no reader can tell whether post-delivery columns entered the model — the one question that determines whether 96 % on DataCo means anything. The only feature evidence (Figs. 15–16) describes a 15-raw-feature model that cannot be the 4-input ANN of Table 1.
3. **Numbers do not survive cross-checking.** Fig. 2 contradicts eq. (16); 97.56 % (p. 22) contradicts Table 6; 94 % (p. 20) contradicts Fig. 14; Fig. 18 contradicts Table 7; Fig. 14 has 1,484 rows more than a 20 % split allows; Table 2 is missing a row the text describes; "default risk and credit rating" (p. 11) is text from another study.

**Credibility: LOW.** The headline accuracy is produced by a selection procedure that reads the test set, on an undisclosed feature set, and the paper's own numbers disagree with one another in at least six places.

**Methodology to reuse.**
- **Nothing from the modelling pipeline.** PCA→SOM→GA-BPNN is not relevant to a LightGBM-backed reliance experiment, and the pipeline here is not evaluated soundly enough to borrow from.
- **One negative lesson worth citing for our evaluation section:** eq. (19)'s fitness `max{train acc, test acc}` is a concrete, citable example of test-set selection leakage in a *Nature*-portfolio paper. Our `NUMBERS.md` discipline (every number tied to a script + seed) is the direct answer.
- **One positive lesson:** reporting train **and** test accuracy side by side (Table 4) is cheap and revealing; our supplement already does the equivalent.

**For the literature review.** Citable as (a) an instance of the DL-for-SCM framework genre, (b) evidence that headline accuracies on DataCo are published without feature-set disclosure or a majority-class floor. Do **not** cite it as a confirmed leakage case, and do **not** repeat its 97.56 % or its Table 6 comparisons.

**What it says about our error-placement design.** Nothing directly — no human is in the loop anywhere in this paper. Its only bearing on reliance is indirect: Figs. 15–16 are the kind of "explanation" a planner would be shown, and here they explain a model that is demonstrably not the one benchmarked. That is a concrete argument for our study's requirement that the displayed risk % be recomputed from the served model (`src/interface/verify_log.py`), rather than trusted.
