Part 1 · The industry standard

What companies check before they trust a model

Banks follow the most formal rules, set by the US Federal Reserve in SR 11-7 (2011). Retail and logistics firms use the same three ideas. First, conceptual soundness: does the model use sensible inputs for sensible reasons? Second, outcomes analysis: compare predictions with what actually happened, on later periods the model never saw (backtesting), and against other models (benchmarking). Third, ongoing monitoring once it is live. Google's ML Test Score (Breck et al. 2017) adds 28 engineering tests, including one that checks the live system gives the same prediction as the offline model. For the paper, TRIPOD+AI (Collins et al. 2024) is the reporting checklist reviewers recognise.

Part 2 · What to add

Six checks, in the order to run them

Each check uses data and code we already have. If a check fails after G0, the fix is to disclose the result in the paper. The model is not retrained.

  1. 1

    Backtest on later weeks, and on held-out states before G0 · Fri 2 Oct

    WhatTrain the live model on the early weeks of every series and test it on the later weeks. Then run a second split: train on two US states and test on the third (California, Texas, Wisconsin; the 70 series come from 10 stores in these 3 states).

    WhyThis is what "backtesting" means in SR 11-7, and it matches the rolling-origin tests in Li (2026) and the hidden later period in M5. At present a reviewer can say the model has only been tested on different series from the same years.

    Pass ifAUROC stays near 0.89 and calibration error stays small. A large drop is itself a finding to report.

  2. 2

    Full calibration report before G0 · Fri 2 Oct

    WhatAdd calibration intercept and slope, and a calibration plot of predicted risk against observed stockout rate, to the existing ECE 0.006.

    WhyParticipants read the risk % as a probability, so it has to mean what it says. Van Calster et al. (2019) call calibration "the Achilles heel" of prediction models. Van den Goorbergh et al. (2022) show that undersampling distorts probabilities, which is why the live model is not undersampled.

    Pass ifThe slope is close to 1 and the intercept close to 0.

  3. 3

    Explain what the model uses (SHAP) before G0 · Fri 2 Oct

    WhatRun SHAP on the live model and list its top features.

    WhyThis is SR 11-7's conceptual soundness check, and our anchor paper did the same. We expect net and current inventory position to lead, because that one feature alone gives AUROC 0.859. If the model leans on something odd, we need to know before participants see it.

    Pass ifThe top features make operational sense: stock position, backorders, recent demand.

  4. 4

    Check the risk % served in each trial before deploying · Wed 7 Oct

    WhatExtend verify_log.py so it recomputes the risk % for every trial from the logged state and compares it with the value shown.

    WhyThe ML Test Score calls this a training–serving skew test. It catches a wrong model file or a LightGBM version mismatch in the Cloud Run container, which would otherwise go unnoticed.

    Pass ifEvery trial matches exactly.

  5. 5

    Does the risk % beat a no-model rule? with the analysis scripts · Fri 9 Oct

    WhatSimulate an ordering rule that adds stock when the risk % is high. Compare its cost and stockouts with the same rule without the model, over the same demand, with bootstrap intervals.

    WhyCompanies judge a model by money and service level, not by AUROC. Li (2026) makes a model beat a no-model rule before counting it as useful, and on Olist the paper's own model failed that test. Decision curve analysis (Vickers & Elkin 2006) is the formal version of this.

    Pass ifThe model-guided rule costs less, with an interval that excludes zero. Whether it helps people is still the experiment's question.

  6. 6

    Stress tests with the analysis scripts · Fri 9 Oct

    WhatRe-score the model with demand scaled by ±30 %, the disruption rate doubled, and more variable lead times.

    WhyThis is SR 11-7's sensitivity analysis. It shows how far the model can be trusted outside the conditions it was trained on, which answers the digital-twin review's warning that most twin studies are never tested outside ideal data.

    Pass ifPerformance falls gradually rather than collapsing. Either way, report the curve.

For the paper itself
  • Fill in the TRIPOD+AI checklist and a leakage "model info sheet" (Kapoor & Narayanan 2023) as supplementary material. Both are cheap and signal rigour to reviewers.
  • Disclose the Ntakolia split. Their text says a 70/30 split, but their confusion matrices add up to 5,588 test rows, which is 20 % of the balanced data. We followed the matrices (80/20, 5,593 rows). The project notes don't record this yet, and a reviewer who reads "70/30" will ask.
  • State that the Kaggle numbers come from a balanced test set. They show ranking ability, not probabilities at the real 0.72 % backorder rate.
Part 3 · The 14 papers

How each paper validated its model

A pattern stands out. The two recent supply-chain papers with the biggest claims (Thaker 2026, Ahmed 2025) have the weakest validation. The strongest work (M5, Li 2026) uses hidden or time-ordered tests with intervals. That is the bar to meet, and item 1 above closes our last gap to it.
