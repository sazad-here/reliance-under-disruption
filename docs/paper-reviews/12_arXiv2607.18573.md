## 1 SNAPSHOT

- **Paper:** Jize Li. "When Does Machine Learning Beat Value Sorting? A Three-Dataset Diagnostic of Exposure-Weighted Shipment Prioritization." arXiv:2607.18573 (2026); ACM ref. format cites *Proceedings of AIIIP 2026*.
- **Problem:** not "is the delay model accurate?" but "under a fixed review budget, does the model's ranking capture more delay exposure than sorting by shipment value?"
- **Method:** define severity `d = max(actual − scheduled, 0)`, exposure `e = d·v`, metric `Capture@k`; compare seven rankers (RANDOM, VALUE_ONLY, PROB_ONLY, M0 severity-only, BINARY×VALUE, M1 severity×value, M2b_tuned cost-sensitive) under five rolling-origin folds with training-fold-only preprocessing and 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples.
- **Data:** SCMS (10,324), DataCo (180,519), Olist (96,470 delivered orders).
- **Headline:** M1 beats M0 everywhere, but clears the VALUE_ONLY gate only on DataCo (+10.1 pp); fails on SCMS (−5.5 pp, CI crosses zero) and Olist (−4.9 pp, CI strictly negative). Cost-sensitive retraining never helps.
- **Code:** promised, not yet available.
- [Assessment] A short, disciplined negative-result paper. The methodology is the contribution, and it holds up.

## 2 ABSTRACT

- **Claims:** (i) M1 > M0 on all three; (ii) M1 > VALUE_ONLY only on DataCo; (iii) the divide tracks severity learnability (R² and calibration bias); (iv) cost-sensitive retraining adds nothing; (v) "Value sorting should remain a permanent benchmark."
- **Evidence promised and delivered:** every abstract number reappears in Table 2 with a CI. The abstract states the protocol (leakage-controlled rolling-origin, 1,000-sample paired bootstrap) rather than just asserting rigour.
- [Assessment] Unusually honest for the genre: the abstract leads with a result that limits the usefulness of the author's own model class. The one soft spot is "The divide is **consistent with** severity learnability" — correctly hedged in the abstract, and kept hedged in §6.3.

## 3 INTRODUCTION AND MOTIVATION

- **Claim (§1):** "Delivery-delay prediction becomes operationally useful only when it changes a constrained decision."
- **Evidence:** framing, not measurement — but the framing is specific: a manager reviews the top k %, so the target quantity is exposure captured in that slice, not AUC.
- **Gap:** "Delivery-delay studies commonly emphasize predictive metrics such as accuracy, AUC, or F1, but an operational baseline and a rolling-origin evaluation under a fixed review budget can change the deployment conclusion [12, 15]."
- **Why VALUE_ONLY is a fair adversary (§1):** "shipment value is known before delivery, directly enters the exposure proxy, and requires no machine-learning model."
- [Assessment] The motivation is exactly the one our study needs stated in the literature: accuracy is not the decision. The paper also names, in §7.1, why the baseline is strong *by construction* — value is a factor in exposure — rather than pretending the comparison is neutral.

## 4 LITERATURE REVIEW / RELATED WORK

- Three short sub-sections: delay prediction as decision support (§2.1), cost-sensitive learning (§2.2, Elkan [6], Zadrozny et al. [16]), boosting/SHAP/ranking evaluation (§2.3, Chen & Guestrin [4], Lundberg & Lee [8], Elmachtoub & Grigas [7], Mandi et al. [9]).
- **Novelty claim (§2.1):** "To our knowledge, no prior study systematically tests delay-prioritization ML against a no-model value-sorting policy across multiple datasets with a learnability/calibration mechanism."
- **Evidence:** the claim is bounded ("to our knowledge") and the adjacent literature it would have to displace — decision-focused learning [7, 9] — is cited rather than ignored.
- [Assessment] 17 references is thin for a full paper but proportionate to a 9-page diagnostic. The related-work section correctly identifies the difference between its contribution and decision-focused learning: DFL changes the *training* objective; this paper changes the *acceptance test*. That distinction is not spelled out explicitly and would be worth making.

## 5 METHODOLOGY

**Formal setup (§3):** `d_i = max(a_i − s_i, 0)` (eq. 1); `e_i = d_i·v_i` (eq. 2); `Capture@k(r) = Σ_{i∈T_k(r)} e_i / Σ_i e_i` (eq. 3); `Δgate = Capture@k(M1) − Capture@k(VALUE_ONLY)` (eq. 4). Gate clears iff the paired 95 % bootstrap CI for Δgate is **strictly positive** — "A positive point estimate without a strictly positive CI is not a passed gate" (§3).

**Leakage control (§5.1):** five outer rolling-origin folds, "each test fold follows its training data"; imputers and one-hot encoders fit within the training fold; Olist's "leakage audit excludes actual delivery timestamp, post-purchase carrier events, delivery status, delay, severity target, and binary-target aliases"; §4.1: "Across datasets, value is known at ranking time; actual delivery timestamps, derived labels, and other post-shipment fields are excluded from model features."

**Model (§5.1):** XGBoost severity regressor, 180 trees, max depth 4, lr 0.05, subsample 0.85, colsample 0.85, L1 0, L2 1. M0/M1/M2b_tuned share hyperparameters; M2b_tuned differs only in training-fold sample weight `w_i(α) = 1 + α·v_i/v_scale` (eq. 5), with `α ∈ {0,1,2,5,10}` chosen by **nested** rolling-origin validation — "The outer test fold never selects α."

**Target transform (§5.2):** raw vs log(1+d) chosen by pooled rolling-origin validation RMSE; raw for SCMS and DataCo, log for Olist. Explicitly flagged: "The comparison is transformation selection within the same model family, not a dataset-specific hyperparameter advantage for M2b_tuned."

**Uncertainty (§5.2):** 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples, `random_state=42`, 95 % percentile intervals.

**Selection-bias correction (§4.2):** the original SCMS pipeline kept 4,592 of 10,324 rows and "removed all From-RDC fulfillment records"; the corrected pipeline keeps all 10,324 including 5,404 From-RDC (52.34 %). "This changes the evaluated population materially and is therefore a selection-bias correction, not merely a missing-value convenience."

**Drift justification (§4.3):** late prevalence by chronological block — SCMS 1.95–16.35 %, Olist 4.46–17.21 %, DataCo 57.04–58.02 %.

**Gaps:**
- **Feature lists.** Only Olist's is enumerated (§4.1). SCMS's and DataCo's are not, though §4.1's blanket exclusion statement covers the leakage question at the level of principle.
- **Fold sizes** are not reported, so the per-fold n behind the bootstrap is unknown.
- **No hyperparameter search** for the severity regressor; one fixed configuration is applied to three very different datasets. §5.1 presents this as a fairness device (shared hyperparameters across M0/M1/M2b), which it is, but it also means a stronger severity model might have cleared the gate on SCMS or Olist. The paper does not test that.
- **A single model family.** XGBoost only. The conclusion "severity is not learnable at planning time on SCMS and Olist" is really "not learnable *by this XGBoost configuration*". §8 acknowledges the data limitation ("retrospective data may lack richer pre-delivery signals") but not the model-capacity one.

- [Assessment] The protocol is the strongest part of the paper and is directly borrowable. Two things in particular: the gate as a *strictly positive CI* rather than a point estimate, and the nested selection of α so that the tuning knob cannot see the outer test fold.

## 6 RESULTS

- **§6.1 Value weighting beats severity-only.** M1 − M0 at k = 10 %: +34.70 pp SCMS (CI [27.38, 40.97]), +11.08 pp DataCo (CI [10.72, 11.46]), +27.11 pp Olist (CI [21.97, 32.32]). All three CIs are strictly positive. "This is the robust positive result: known value matters at ranking time."
- **§6.2 The gate.** Cleared only on DataCo. SCMS: negative point estimate, CI crosses zero. Olist: −4.87 pp, CI strictly negative — a *demonstrated* loss, not an inconclusive one. "Across the stored 5%, 10%, and 20% comparisons, M1 exceeds VALUE_ONLY only on DataCo" — the 5 % and 20 % numbers are not tabulated.
- **§6.3 Mechanism.** DataCo ρ = 0.386, R² = 0.270, bias +0.010 days; SCMS ρ = 0.149, R² = −0.021, bias −1.658; Olist ρ = 0.075, R² = −0.019, bias −0.611. Negative R² means the severity regressor is worse than predicting the mean — a strong and honestly reported result.
- **§6.4 Cost-sensitive retraining.** −3.33 pp SCMS (CI [−9.21, −0.59]), −0.04 pp DataCo (CI [−0.09, 0.01]), −3.03 pp Olist (CI [−4.86, −1.37]). DataCo is correctly described as a *precise* non-improvement, with the CI half-width (0.05 pp) given so the reader can judge power — a better practice than declaring a null.
- **§6.5 Full ranker comparison** (Table 4). PROB_ONLY is poor everywhere (.237 / .108 / .150), barely above RANDOM on DataCo.
- **§6.6 Operational diagnostics.** Median M1-ranked warning lead time: 108 days SCMS, **2.00 days DataCo**, 22.09 days Olist. SHAP leaders: SCMS PQ-first-sent date and project code; DataCo shipment mode and planned duration; Olist estimated delivery days and purchase month.
- **§6.7 Binary vs severity.** BINARY×VALUE beats M1 on SCMS, loses on DataCo, ties on Olist. "Candidate policies should therefore be compared at the deployment budget rather than selected from model-family labels."

**What the evidence supports vs what is claimed:**
- Supported: all Δgate and Δ(M2b−M1) statements, the M1 > M0 result, and the calibration description (Fig. 1 shows actual severity above the diagonal for SCMS and Olist, i.e. underestimation, matching the negative bias).
- Correctly hedged: the mechanism. The paper never says learnability *causes* gate clearance; §6.3 says learnability and calibration co-vary and declines to separate them.
- **A tension the paper does not resolve:** §6.6 notes that on DataCo the SHAP leaders are "shipment mode and planned duration", and that "DataCo's learnability plausibly reflects standardized shipment modes that expose a clean, low-cardinality delay signal at order time". Combined with §6.6's 2.00-day median warning lead time, this suggests DataCo's severity is learnable partly because it is *near-deterministic given shipment mode* — the same property that makes DataCo's binary late label a two-column rule. The paper treats DataCo's learnability as the favourable case without asking whether it is favourable because the dataset is close to synthetic. That is a genuine gap, not an error.

- **Table 1 (p. 4) Dataset scope.** SCMS 10,324/10,324/1,186; DataCo 180,519/180,519/103,400; Olist 99,441/96,470/7,826. Clean and arithmetically consistent with §4.1–4.3. Notable: DataCo loses **zero** rows to label-validity filtering while Olist loses 2,971 — consistent with DataCo's labels being computed rather than observed.
- **Table 2 (p. 5) Three-dataset diagnostic.** Eight columns: ρ, R², M0 lift, bias (days), VALUE capture, M1 capture, M1−VALUE with 95 % CI, Gate?. The single most useful table in the paper: it puts the diagnostic quantities and the decision in one row per dataset. M0 lift reconciles with Table 4 (lift = capture/0.10) in all three rows.
- **Figure 1 (p. 6) Severity calibration by predicted-severity decile.** Three panels, mean actual vs mean predicted severity, with a perfect-calibration diagonal. DataCo's points sit essentially on the diagonal from 0.5 to 2.0 days. SCMS's are far above it and non-monotone (a dip near predicted ≈ 1 before jumping to ≈ 5.5). Olist's are above the diagonal and non-monotone (a spike at predicted ≈ 0.2 reaching actual ≈ 1.25). Legible and it supports the caption. **What it also shows, and the text does not say:** SCMS and Olist calibration curves are *non-monotone*, which is a rank problem, not only a bias problem — arguably stronger support for the paper's own "recalibration alone cannot rescue M1" argument (§6.3) than the bias numbers are.
- **Table 3 (p. 6) M2b_tuned − M1.** Three rows, difference and CI. Consistent with §6.4.
- **Table 4 (p. 7) Master results, Capture@10 %.** Seven rankers × three datasets. RANDOM gives .079 / .100 / .097 — the sanity check a reader wants (RANDOM ≈ k = 0.10 when exposure is not concentrated; SCMS's .079 indicates exposure concentration there). No CIs in this table; they live in Tables 2–3 and §6.7, which means several of Table 4's pairwise gaps cannot be judged from the table alone.

## 8 DISCUSSION

- **§7.1:** "Value sorting needs no model and is strong by construction: value is one factor in exposure itself. M1 must contribute severity ordering beyond what value already encodes."
- **§7.2:** the operational reading — on DataCo, 22 % → 32 % capture means "the same queue reaches almost half again as much delayed value"; on SCMS, M1 "forfeit[s] exposure that a database-column sort would catch". Then a five-step deployment checklist.
- **§7.3:** why retraining need not help — "a calibrated severity estimate multiplied by known value is already a ranking-time expected-exposure rule. Sample weighting can distort severity ordering without contributing new information." Scope is then narrowed explicitly to "cost-proportionate example weighting with a nested-CV-selected weight".
- **§7.4:** generalisation to fraud screening, predictive maintenance, collections — "compare ML with sorting by known magnitude under a fixed budget."
- [Assessment] The discussion argues from the results and bounds itself at every step. §7.4 is the only speculative passage and is labelled as such. The asymmetry argument in §7.2 ("a model that helps in one setting can quietly cost capture in another, and only a value-only comparison makes that visible") is the paper's sharpest sentence and the one most worth citing.

## 9 LIMITATIONS

The paper's own (§8): three datasets cannot identify a universal or causal threshold; retrospective data may lack richer pre-delivery signals; severity×value is "an interpretable proxy, not every downstream cost"; because the proxy is multiplicative, "value sorting is strong by construction" and "under nonlinear or threshold delay penalties … the gate verdict could differ"; SHAP explains association not causation; warning lead time is a dataset-specific analog; calibration and ranking need drift monitoring.

- **Not acknowledged:** (a) a single model family and a single fixed hyperparameter set, so "not learnable" is model-relative; (b) DataCo's severity may be learnable because the dataset is close to rule-generated (see §6 above); (c) the 5 % and 20 % budget results are asserted but not shown; (d) fold sizes and per-fold variability are not reported.
- [Assessment] The limitations section is substantive rather than ritual — it names the specific condition (multiplicative cost model) under which the paper's own conclusion would flip. The unacknowledged items are real but second-order.

## 10 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

- **§9:** restates the three-dataset pattern and the deployment rule. Consistent with the abstract and with Tables 2–4; no number appears in the conclusion that is not in a table.
- **Future work:** §7.3 names it — "asymmetric losses, quantile or distributional regression, or other cost structures".
- [Assessment] No inflation between results and conclusion. This is the cleanest abstract↔results↔conclusion chain of the three papers reviewed in this batch.

## 12 CRITICAL EVALUATION

**Strengths (max 3)**
1. **The acceptance test is the contribution, and it is correctly specified.** A strictly-positive paired bootstrap CI against a no-model baseline (§3, eq. 4) is a higher bar than anything in the surrounding literature, and the paper applies it to its own model and reports two failures out of three.
2. **Leakage and selection bias are handled explicitly and named as such.** Rolling-origin folds with training-fold-only preprocessing (§5.1), an enumerated exclusion list (§5.1), nested selection of α (§5.1), and the SCMS correction that restores 5,732 dropped rows including all From-RDC records (§4.2).
3. **Claims are bounded at every step.** Three explicit refusals to over-claim (§5.2, §6.2, §6.3) and a limitations section that names the assumption (multiplicative exposure) under which the headline conclusion would change.

**Weaknesses (max 3)**
1. **One model family, one hyperparameter configuration.** "Severity is not learnable" on SCMS and Olist is established only for a fixed 180-tree, depth-4 XGBoost. The paper does not test whether a better severity model clears the gate, and §8 does not list this among its threats.
2. **DataCo's favourable case is not interrogated.** §6.6 notes DataCo's signal comes from "standardized shipment modes" and a 2.00-day median lead time; Table 1 shows DataCo loses zero rows to label-validity filtering. The one dataset where ML wins is plausibly the one whose labels are most rule-like, and the paper does not raise this.
3. **Incomplete reporting of the budget sweep and folds.** The 5 % and 20 % comparisons are asserted (§6.2) but not shown; fold sizes and per-fold results are absent, so the reader cannot see whether the pooled bootstrap hides fold-level instability. Code is not yet released.

**Credibility: HIGH.** Every cross-checkable number in the paper reconciles (the sole exception is a rounding-level CI precision mismatch in §2), the protocol is specified well enough that we independently reproduced its DataCo headline (0.322 vs 0.323), and the paper's main result runs against the author's own interest.

**Methodology to reuse — this is the most directly useful of the three papers.**

1. **A no-model gate for our own decision claim.** Our `Fig14_decision_value` compares the model rule against a base-stock rule and a net-position top-up. That is structurally Li's gate. We should say so and cite it: the reason our comparison is "+464 [331, 583] against the best *no-model* rule" rather than "against doing nothing" is exactly §7.1's argument. Our CI is already strictly positive, so we pass our own version of the gate.
2. **"A positive point estimate without a strictly positive CI is not a passed gate" (§3).** Worth adopting verbatim as a reporting rule for the behavioural results, where effect sizes will be small.
3. **The precise-null presentation (§6.4).** When we report a non-difference (e.g. between error placements, if one appears), report the CI half-width so the reader can judge power, rather than declaring no effect.
4. **Nested selection (§5.1)** — our hyperparameters must not be chosen on anything the final number is computed from. Our `NUMBERS.md` discipline should record this explicitly for the live model.
5. **Do not reuse** the exposure proxy `severity × value` for our task; our decision is a stockout top-up, not a review queue, and §8 warns the gate verdict is conditional on the multiplicative cost model.
